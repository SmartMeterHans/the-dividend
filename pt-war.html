Plain Truth 01 — Wars & Their Cost

What wars
actually cost —
and what we
could buy instead

Not in political speeches. Not in defence budgets. In real things — hospitals, schools, clean water, children who grow up. Here are the numbers no one puts on a poster.

Right now, while you're reading this, there are eight major wars happening on earth. Sudan. Ukraine. Gaza. Yemen. Myanmar. Congo. Somalia. The Sahel. Every single one of them is costing money that could be doing something else.

Not a little money. Enormous amounts of money.

The war in Sudan — which most people in Europe and America couldn't find on a map — is costing around $80 million every single day. It started in April 2023. Do the maths. That's over $60 billion gone, just for one conflict that most people aren't paying attention to.

The Sudan Numbers
$80 million. Every day.
150,000 people have died. 11 million have fled their homes — more than fled Syria at the height of that war. 1.73 million have crossed international borders. The daily cost of $80 million is a conservative estimate combining direct military costs, infrastructure destruction, economic collapse, and humanitarian response. The total since April 2023: over $60 billion.

Here's what I want you to think about. That $60 billion didn't just disappear. It was real money, real resources, real human effort — pointed at destruction instead of construction.

So let's talk about what $60 billion could have done instead.

It's not just Sudan.

Add up all eight active conflicts. The total annual cost — direct military spending, humanitarian response, economic damage, reconstruction — runs to hundreds of billions of dollars every single year.

And that's before you count the global military budget. The world spends $2.3 trillion on defence every year. To put that in perspective: the entire United Nations annual budget is about $3 billion. We spend more on weapons in 36 hours than the UN gets for a whole year.

Why does this affect you, even if you're not there?

Two reasons.

First: the people fleeing these wars have to go somewhere. Over 46 million people are currently displaced by conflict worldwide. When they arrive in Europe, America, or neighbouring countries, that creates pressure — on housing, on services, on politics. The cost of hosting displaced people in the EU alone is estimated at €43 billion a year. That is not free money. It comes from somewhere.

Second: the money spent on these wars is money that cannot be spent on climate change, pandemic preparedness, or the dozens of other global problems that will eventually arrive at your door regardless of where you live.

A note on what "cost" means
These numbers are conservative.
All figures in this analysis use the lower end of credible estimates. The true costs — when you include lost economic growth, generational trauma, environmental damage, and long-term health impacts — are substantially higher. We use conservative numbers precisely because we don't need to exaggerate. The conservative numbers are damning enough.

Here's the bit that matters.

Wars are not natural disasters. They don't just happen. They are funded. They are equipped. They involve decisions made by governments, arms companies, and individuals who weigh up whether the cost is worth it.

For most of history, those decisions have been made with very sophisticated analysis on the war side and almost no analysis on the peace side. Nobody builds a spreadsheet showing what 500 days of peace in Sudan would buy. Nobody models the GDP of a country that didn't go to war.

We do. That's what this project is. We're not anti-military. We're pro-arithmetic.
