Plain Truth 02 — The AI Choice

The most powerful
technology ever built.
What are we
using it for?

AI could cure diseases your doctor has never heard of. It could end hunger. It could find clean water for a billion people. Instead, we're spending 43 times more making weapons smarter. This is not inevitable. It's a choice.

In 2020, a team at DeepMind — which is owned by Google — released something called AlphaFold. It solved one of the hardest problems in biology: predicting how proteins fold. Scientists had been trying to crack that problem for 50 years. AlphaFold did it in months.

The result? A freely available database of over 200 million protein structures. Drug researchers around the world now use it every day. It has already accelerated work on malaria, Parkinson's, antibiotic resistance, and dozens of other diseases. Some researchers say it's the most important biological tool ever created.

The total cost to develop AlphaFold: approximately $10 million.

Let that sink in
$10 million changed drug discovery for all of humanity.
For context: a single US military drone programme costs around $2.5 billion. The US Department of Defense's total AI budget in 2024 was $17.9 billion. That's 1,790 AlphaFolds. We chose not to build them.

Where the money actually goes.

Global investment in military AI — autonomous targeting systems, drone swarms, cyber warfare tools, missile guidance — reached roughly $96 billion in 2024. That figure is growing at around 24% per year.

Global investment in humanitarian AI — disease research, food security, clean water, disaster response — is harder to pin down precisely, but credible estimates put it at around $2 billion.

That's a ratio of $43 spent on military AI for every $1 spent on AI for human welfare.

Here's what the other side of that equation looks like.

These are real comparisons between military AI programmes and humanitarian AI projects that exist or could exist with equivalent funding.

What we're funding
Autonomous drone targeting AI
$6B annual investment (US alone)
AI systems that identify and engage targets without human confirmation. Multiple active programmes across US, China, Russia, Israel, Turkey, and others.
What that money could fund
ALS early detection & treatment AI
Full global research budget: ~$100M/yr
ALS kills everyone who gets it. There is no cure. The entire global research budget is 60× smaller than one drone targeting programme. Equivalent investment would likely produce a treatment within 10 years.
What we're funding
AI disinformation & influence operations
$4.2B estimated global spend
Military and intelligence AI designed to generate fake news, manipulate social media, and destabilise political systems. Every major power is doing this.
What that money could fund
Global food system optimisation
Full programme cost: ~$800M one-time
AI that optimises crop yields, predicts famines 18 months in advance, and routes food aid to where it's needed before crises escalate. 800 million people are chronically hungry. This is solvable.
What we're funding
Hypersonic missile guidance AI
$3.8B US programme alone
AI systems to guide missiles travelling at Mach 5+ through unpredictable trajectories. Each missile costs $50–100 million. The AI to guide it costs more than that to develop.
What that money could fund
Clean water detection for rural communities
Full global deployment: ~$500M
AI that identifies safe groundwater sources and optimises purification. 2.2 billion people lack safe drinking water. The technology to find and deliver it already exists. It just isn't funded.
We are not short of the technology to solve these problems. We are not short of the science. We are short of the decision to apply one to the other.

But isn't military AI necessary for national security?

That's a legitimate question. We're not going to pretend it isn't.

Here's what we'd say: even if you believe some military AI investment is justified, the current ratio of 43 to 1 is not a carefully reasoned strategic balance. It is the output of budget processes designed decades ago, dominated by defence contractors whose business model depends on continuous military investment.

Nobody sat down and decided: "43 times more military AI than humanitarian AI is the correct number." It just happened. And things that just happen can be changed.

The possibility
A 10-to-1 ratio would change everything.
We're not asking for the ratio to flip. We're asking: what if it went from 43-to-1 to 10-to-1? That would still mean far more military AI than humanitarian. But the humanitarian share would increase by 330%. That's the difference between a cure for one disease and a cure for thirty. That's the difference between one AlphaFold and a hundred. The technology is ready. The question is whether the decision is.
